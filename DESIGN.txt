POKEMON TCG RESTOCK TRACKER - DESIGN NOTES
Written 2026-09-21. Companion to README.md, which covers setup and day-to-day use.
Facts marked (observed) are requests the research made on 2026-09-21. Sources are in section 9.


1. RECOMMENDATION

Build it on a report log, and check automatically only where a store publishes its own stock data.

  - Backbone: reports you, and people you trust, log. The site pre-fills a GitHub issue form, and
    an Action validates the answers and appends them to data/reports.json. Last restock and next
    expected are computed from that log.
    It is the only approach that works for every store, Target, Walmart and GameStop included. It
    is inside everyone's terms. And it is the only one that yields restock timing at all: no
    retailer publishes restock schedules, and a stock check only ever tells you "now".
  - Add-on for local game stores that sell through Shopify: the page asks each tracked product's
    public JSON live when you open it. An hourly Action asks too and records changes, so those
    stores get a restock history without you logging anything.
  - Not built: scraping Target, Walmart or GameStop; pulling from community trackers or Discord;
    Best Buy's API. Section 2 has the reasons.

  Why this suits a solo hobby project: nothing to host or pay for, no API keys, and no scraper to
  repair every time a retailer changes its site. The one recurring task is logging what you see,
  which is also what makes the forecast work.


2. THE OPTIONS COMPARED

  A. Scraping big-box retailers (Target, Walmart, GameStop)
     Gives:       Online and pickup availability per store. Target's unofficial RedSky
                  "fulfillment" endpoint answers per store. It never gives restock timing.
     Reliability: Poor, especially from GitHub's servers.
                  - Target and Walmart run HUMAN/PerimeterX bot detection, which blocks
                    datacenter IPs quickly.
                  - A single plain request to Walmart got its "Robot or human?" challenge, and
                    GameStop's homepage returned a Cloudflare 403 (both observed).
                  - RedSky needs a key embedded in target.com, which has changed at least once,
                    plus a per-session visitor id.
     Terms:       All three forbid it in so many words.
                  - Target (updated 2026-09-10) bans "data extraction, scraping, mining or other
                    data gathering tools" and unapproved queries "through API call(s)".
                  - Walmart (2026-09-09) prohibits "data mining, robots, or similar data
                    gathering and extraction tools".
                  - GameStop bans spiders, robots and scraping.
     Upkeep:      High. It breaks whenever the page, the key or the bot rules change.
     Verdict:     No. Instead, give each product a "View" link to the retailer's page (the
                  `items` field). Checking store availability there by hand is ordinary use,
                  and you log what you see.

  B. Retailer APIs
     Best Buy:    A real, documented in-store availability endpoint:
                  /v1/products/{sku}/stores.json. It lists the stores that have stock, with a
                  lowStock flag, allows 5 calls a second and 50,000 a day, and Best Buy does sell
                  Pokemon TCG in its stores. Three problems:
                  - Since 2016 Best Buy has refused keys to free email addresses such as Gmail
                    and Yahoo.
                  - Whether individuals can get keys in 2026 couldn't be confirmed.
                  - The API terms tie use to selling Best Buy's products.
                  Not built. If you do get a key, a "bestbuy" checker next to the Shopify one in
                  scripts/restock.py is about 15 lines.
     Walmart:     The affiliate API exists for referral fees, and its store-level stock needs
                  extra approval from Walmart's business team. No.

  C. Community trackers (BrickSeek, NowInStock, HotStock, TrackaLacker, PokeNotify)
     None offers a public API, feed or webhook. The search wasn't exhaustive.
     - BrickSeek, NowInStock and HotStock all forbid bots, scraping or reproduction.
     - BrickSeek now puts every page behind a sign-in wall (observed).
     - The paid tiers ($3 to $8 a month) send alerts to you; they don't supply data to your app.
     Verdict: not a data source. Use them alongside this site for online drops.

  D. Discord restock servers
     - Webhooks can only post into a channel; nothing can be read back from them.
     - You could follow an Announcement channel into your own server and read it with your own
       bot, but only if the source server publishes one. The bot also needs hosting or a polling
       job, and the alerts are mostly online drops, not your local shelves.
     - Self-bots are banned, and Discord's terms forbid scraping.
     Verdict: no.

  E. Shopify storefronts (many local game stores)
     Gives:       Every Shopify store serves /products/<handle>.js with "available" for the
                  product and for each variant. The response is readable cross-origin, so both
                  a browser and an Action can ask (both observed, and verified in this build).
     Reliability: Good from a browser. From GitHub's servers, some stores' Cloudflare answers
                  429; tcg-restock-watcher, an open-source tracker built the same way, hits
                  this. GitHub's cron also runs late.
     Terms:       Grey rather than black.
                  - Shopify's own terms bind merchants and API developers, and ban automated
                    collection and building a product index. Whether they bind an anonymous
                    visitor is unclear, and each store's terms differ.
                  - One request per tracked product per hour, with an honest User-Agent, is
                    far below what a store would notice. The in-page check is the same request
                    your browser makes when you view the product.
                  - If a store objects or blocks it, remove its "check". Many local stores will
                    also just tell you their restock days if you ask.
     Upkeep:      Low. The endpoint is part of every Shopify theme and has been stable for
                  years. You keep the product URLs current.
     Verdict:     Yes, opt-in per store.

  F. Manual / crowdsourced log
     Gives:       The only source of restock timing, for every kind of store.
     Reliability: As good as the logging. The forecast needs "out" reports as well as "in".
     Terms:       Nothing to breach.
     Upkeep:      A few taps per store visit, and no code to fix.
     Verdict:     Yes, as the backbone.

     Write paths considered for it:
     - A GitHub issue form. Chosen: it's free, needs a sign-in, sits next to the data, leaves an
       audit trail, and an Action validates it.
     - A Google Form feeding a published sheet. Friends wouldn't need GitHub accounts, but the
       data would live in two places. Add it if you want reporters without GitHub.
     - A write token in the page. Anyone could lift it and write to the repo. Ruled out.


3. ARCHITECTURE

  Browser (phone or desktop)                    GitHub (one public repo)
  --------------------------                    ------------------------
  index.html, app.js, restock.js  <-- fetch --  data/products.json, stores.json, reports.json
    |        |                                  (published by .github/workflows/pages.yml)
    |        +-- live, Shopify stores only --> <store>/products/<handle>.js
    |
    +-- "Log" link: pre-filled issue form
          |
          v
  Issue opened -- issues event --> ingest.yml: scripts/restock.py ingest --+-- commit to main
  Every hour ---- schedule ------> check.yml:  scripts/restock.py check  --+   then start
                                     GET <store>/products/<handle>.js          pages.yml

  Frontend: plain HTML and CSS, one ES module for the UI (app.js), and the pure logic
  (restock.js). No framework and no build step. Everything the site shows is computed in the
  browser from the three JSON files, plus the live Shopify answers.

  Deploying: pages.yml publishes the repo with actions/deploy-pages. Your own pushes start it.
  GitHub's docs say a push made with the workflow token doesn't start a Pages build, although two
  repos showed it doing so in September 2026. Rather than rely on either, the two data workflows
  start the deploy explicitly after they push (workflow_dispatch is the documented exception).

  Write path: a static site can't write anywhere without a server or an exposed token. A GitHub
  issue form is the free, signed-in write path that already sits next to the data:
  - The site builds a URL that pre-fills the store and products. GitHub can pre-fill text fields
    only, so you pick the status yourself.
  - The ingest Action validates the answers and commits them.
  - The closed issue stays behind as an audit trail.

  Automated path: the page asks Shopify live on every view (cached for that page view). The
  check Action asks hourly and appends a report only when a product's answer changes. Errors are
  skipped, never recorded as "out".

  Concurrency: both data workflows push to main. They don't use a concurrency group, because
  GitHub cancels all but the newest queued run in a group, which would drop reports. Instead each
  one re-reads the latest main, redoes its change and pushes, retrying if it loses a race. Ingest
  is idempotent: every report carries its issue number and is never logged twice. The retry was
  tested against a local repo whose hook rejects the first push.

  The scripts use only Python's standard library, so the Actions install nothing and there are
  no dependencies to keep updated.


4. DATA MODEL

  data/products.json  [{id, set, type}]  seeded list, newest set first
  data/stores.json    [{id, name, lat, lng, chain?, address?, carries?, notes?, items?, check?}]
                      ships empty; you fill it in (the README has the field table)
  data/reports.json   append-only log, one report per line:
                      {at (UTC ISO), store, product, status, source, by?, ref?, expected?, note?}
                      status: in_stock | low | out | restocked
                      source: manual (a logged issue) or shopify (the hourly check)

  Worked out in the browser, never stored:
    current status   = the latest report for that store and product, or the live Shopify answer
    last restock     = the latest "restocked", or the first in-stock or low report after an
                       "out"; reports less than a day apart count once
    next expected    = a date staff gave ("expected") until it passes; otherwise the next of the
                       store's stated restockDays; otherwise last restock plus the median gap
                       between restocks (needs two or more)
    usual weekday    = set once there are 3+ restocks and half or more fall on one weekday
    whole-store line = the same, pooled over every product at the store, since vendors restock
                       everything at once

  Which stores carry a product: every store, unless its `carries` list (product types or ids)
  leaves the product out. An item URL, or any report of the product, overrides the list.


5. LOCATION

  - "Use my location" is the browser's own geolocation API: no service, no key.
  - A ZIP code is looked up once with OpenStreetMap Nominatim, straight from the browser, and the
    result is kept in localStorage. A normal visit makes no lookup at all. That fits Nominatim's
    policy (user-triggered, cached, attribution shown in the footer). api.zippopotam.us is a
    drop-in alternative if Nominatim ever turns you away.
  - Stores carry their own coordinates in stores.json, copied from Google Maps once.
  - Distance is the great-circle formula in restock.js, filtered by the radius picker.

  Skipped: geocoding store addresses automatically. Copying coordinates is one step per store;
  write a script if you ever track dozens.


6. SECURITY

  - The issue body is untrusted input. No workflow pastes issue text into a shell command:
    restock.py reads the event JSON itself and checks every field against stores.json,
    products.json and the fixed status names. Only the issue number, an integer, reaches the
    shell.
  - Only issues from the owner, members and collaborators are taken as soon as they're opened.
    Anyone else's waits for the "approved" label, which only people with triage rights can add.
  - Each workflow's token gets only the permissions it needs: contents write, issues write for
    ingest, actions write to start the deploy, and pages/id-token for the deploy itself.
  - The page escapes every string from the data files before inserting it, and only turns
    http(s) URLs into links.
  - There are no API keys anywhere.


7. KNOWN LIMITS

  - Stock data is only as fresh as the last report or live answer. Reports older than three days
    are greyed out.
  - The forecast is a median gap. That works for stores on a steady vendor schedule and poorly
    for erratic ones, and it needs "out" reports as well as "in" to see a restock at all.
  - Shopify's "available" is online stock, and it counts preorders as in stock.
  - GitHub starts scheduled workflows late (hours, on busy days) and occasionally drops a run.
    It also pauses them after 60 days without repository activity. check.yml re-enables itself
    on every run to reset that clock. That trick comes from liskin/gh-workflow-keepalive, not
    from GitHub's docs.
  - Everything in a public repo is public, including who reported what and when (README, Privacy).
  - Not tested live, because no repo exists yet: GitHub rendering and pre-filling the issue form,
    the gh CLI calls (comment, close, starting a workflow), and the Pages deploy. Everything else
    was run: both test suites, the page in a browser, the ingest shell loop against a local git
    remote, and the checker against a live Shopify store.


8. WHAT YOU HAVE TO SUPPLY

  - Your stores. data/stores.json ships empty on purpose: nothing in this repo pretends to know
    which stores are near you, where they are, or when they restock.
  - Product page URLs, only for "View" links and Shopify checks.
  - A check of data/products.json. It was compiled from the sources below on 2026-09-21. It
    includes sets released after this model's training data (Chaos Rising, Pitch Black,
    30th Celebration) and one not yet out: Delta Reign, 2026-11-06, with its products taken from
    preorder listings. The 30th Celebration booster bundle ships 2026-10-02.
  - The GitHub repo itself, and the decision to make it public (README, Privacy).
